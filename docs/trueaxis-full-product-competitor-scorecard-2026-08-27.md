# TrueAxis HQ — Full Product Scope and Competitor Scorecard

**Prepared:** August 27, 2026  
**Assessment type:** Evidence-led product scope, engineering-quality, and market-context review.  
**Direct comparison set:** HoneyBook, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Bonsai, and Dubsado.

> **This is not a market-share ranking, a customer-satisfaction study, an independent security assessment, legal advice, or a claim that TrueAxis HQ is ready to replace every established platform.** The scores rate the present application against documented product evidence and its recorded validation state. They deliberately discount capabilities that require a real provider, authorized receiver, or authenticated user-session walkthrough.

## Executive assessment

TrueAxis HQ has a **9.0 / 10 build-quality and product-depth score** in this evidence-limited review. It is now a broad, coherent service-business operating application rather than a narrow CRM prototype. Its strongest areas are the connection between client intake, booking, jobs, private work context, proof, proposals, invoices, cost review, owner planning, and explicit owner/client data boundaries.

The score is **not a launch certification**. The principal deductions remain external: verified sender/domain and transactional email behavior, controlled Stripe Checkout and signed webhooks, an authorized third-party webhook receiver, native calendar synchronization, authenticated owner/staff/client mobile journeys, and independent accessibility/security assessment. These are real deployment-quality gates, not cosmetic caveats.

| Overall frame | Rating / 10 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Product and engineering scope | **9.0** | Deep, integrated implemented workflows with 95 deterministic test files / 254 tests, strict TypeScript, production build, and bundle budgets in the latest code validation. |
| External operating evidence | **6.4** | Important provider and real-session proof remains intentionally open. |
| Market readiness today | **7.4** | A strong beta/controlled-rollout candidate after the external gates are worked through; not yet a substantiated universal replacement claim. |

## Scope verified in the current build

TrueAxis HQ currently includes a substantial owner workflow: client CRM, structured services, intake and booking, job lifecycle and worklists, client-safe proof and messaging, proposals and approvals, invoices and private job costing, expenses and time, recurring service planning, dashboards and exports, staff access foundations, Team Operations, Dispatch Board, Field Mode, and owner-only integration/webhook and launch-readiness surfaces. It also now has private customer assets, job links, reusable inspection templates, immutable template revisions, saved inspection-response snapshots, private asset service history, and owner-managed staff availability/capacity context.

The application’s design decisions should be read narrowly. Asset service history is observational; inspection response data is private; availability blocks are owner planning data. These features are **not** maintenance automation, inventory, warranties, GPS tracking, payroll/attendance, native calendar synchronization, offline field operation, client delivery, or regulatory compliance.

| Verified control or quality signal | Current evidence | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering regression controls | 95 Vitest files / 254 tests; strict TypeScript; production build; defined bundle budgets passed. | Full browser E2E coverage, production load behavior, or absence of all future defects. |
| Owner/client isolation | Owner-scoped procedures, final owner predicates, minimized portal projections, private asset and inspection records excluded from portal flows. | An independent penetration test or a formal compliance certification. |
| Public experience | Desktop and 375px reviews of core public and invalid-link recovery routes; a mobile install prompt now suppresses itself where public inputs, forms, search, or selection controls exist. | Authenticated owner, staff, and client-device accessibility validation. |
| Delivery recovery | Encrypted durable Stripe-event ledger; encrypted durable outbound workflow-webhook records, bounded attempts, terminal evidence, and manual due-processing. | Successful third-party receipt, payment completion, scheduled recovery execution, or receiver-side idempotency. |

## Scoring method

Each dimension is rated from **0 to 10** using weighted evidence rather than feature count. A score near 9 means the workflow is substantially implemented, connected to adjacent workflows, and validated by current local evidence; it does **not** mean flawless. Scores fall when provider behavior, real-user operation, broad accessibility, independent review, or ecosystem maturity is not demonstrated.

The weighted calculation is **896.5 / 100 = 8.965**, rounded to **9.0 / 10**. This is an update from the August 26 scorecard, crediting the later private inspection, asset, availability/capacity, durable delivery, and public-entry-context work while preserving all external deductions.

## What to build or validate next

The most valuable remaining work is not indiscriminate feature volume. It is disciplined closure of the gates that increase real customer confidence and let TrueAxis truthfully improve its weakest scores.

| Priority | Workstream | Expected score benefit | Why it matters | Evidence required before claiming it |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Controlled SMTP and verified sender/domain | Communications, reliability, launch readiness | Converts application messaging from configured UI to demonstrated delivery. | Verified sender/domain, received transactional message, failure handling, and owner-facing delivery evidence. |
| 2 | Stripe sandbox Checkout and signed webhook walkthrough | Payments, reliability, launch readiness | Turns payment routes and durable recovery code into real provider evidence. | Claimed sandbox, test Checkout, verified return, signed event, replay/idempotency observation. |
| 3 | Authenticated owner/staff/client desktop and 375px journeys | UX, accessibility, launch readiness | Tests the workflows users actually use, including Field Mode and data isolation. | Separate role sessions, keyboard/contrast/error recovery checks, mobile evidence, and recorded findings. |
| 4 | Authorized HTTPS receiver for outbound webhooks | Integrations, reliability | Validates that encrypted durable delivery reaches a real controlled receiver and that the receiver handles idempotency. | HMAC verification, event ID dedupe, deliberate failure/retry, terminal-state evidence. |
| 5 | Native calendar synchronization | Integrations, scheduling | Closes the gap between calendar authorization/feed capability and real incremental synchronization. | Conflict policy, durable sync state, pagination/deletions, invalid-token recovery, disconnect/error visibility. |
| 6 | Field execution maturity | Field-operations competitiveness | Addresses the widest gap versus Jobber and Housecall Pro. | Explicit product decision and privacy policy for any GPS/routing/offline work; device and user validation before claim. |
| 7 | Curated integrations | Ecosystem competitiveness | Increases buyer fit without bloating scope. | Least-privilege scopes, signed webhook checks, durable retries, audit controls, and provider-specific testing. |

## Bottom line

TrueAxis HQ is **stronger than the earlier baseline**: it now has a more connected service-operations model, material privacy boundaries, resilient outbound-event foundations, and better public mobile behavior. It is not yet substantiated as a superior or universal replacement for HoneyBook, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Bonsai, or Dubsado. The clearest path to meaningful competitive improvement is to validate the real provider and authenticated-session seams—rather than making broader untested claims.
